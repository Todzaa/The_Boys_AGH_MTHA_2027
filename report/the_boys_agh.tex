\begin{abstract}
We label every frame of FC Barcelona -- Athletic Club as home possession (H), away possession (A) or dead ball (D)
using only the organisers' automatic player and ball tracking (ATD). The method is a transparent, rule-based pipeline
in NumPy and pandas, with no machine learning, no video and no GPU. It filters and smooths the ball track, assigns
ball control to the nearest player within 2\,m when no rival is equally close, detects ball touches from changes of
ball velocity, detects dead ball from the ball leaving (or being projected to leave) the pitch and from a collective
drop in player speed, and tracks possession with a decaying turnover counter that ignores single touches and keeps
loose balls with the last team in control. We validated the design choices on two public Metrica Sports matches,
both with their original tracking and with the ball degraded to resemble ATD. On the
original tracking our pipeline reaches a spellF1 of 0.757 and 0.768, against 0.438 and 0.415 for the organisers'
baseline; with the ATD-like ball it reaches 0.535 and 0.517, against 0.365 and 0.327. On the public five-minute window
of the competition match, used only as a sanity check, it reaches a spellF1 of 0.563 against the baseline's
0.326. The whole match is labelled deterministically in under a minute on a laptop CPU.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Initial Challenge of More Than a Hack 2027 asks for a frame-by-frame description of ball possession in one LaLiga
match, FC Barcelona against Athletic Club. For each of the 199\,319 frames of the tactical video (25 frames per second)
the submission assigns one label: H when the home team has the ball in play, A when the away team has it, D when the
ball is dead (throw-ins, corners, fouls, goals, other stoppages), and X when no decision is made. Only frames of actual
play are graded: 11\,185--90\,651 in the first half and 110\,808--190\,197 in the second.

The organisers provide automatic player and ball tracking, Smart Tagging of tactical phases, a per-frame camera
homography and the match videos. None of this data is corrected by hand: tracks break and swap identities, and the
ball is missing in about 41\% of the graded frames. The task is therefore not tracking itself, but inferring possession
from noisy positional data.

The ranking metric is spellF1, which rewards recovering whole possession spells with correct boundaries and penalises
fragmented sequences; frame-level macroF1 is used only to break ties. The organisers' baseline (nearest player to the
ball with majority voting) reaches a spellF1 of 0.2433 on the full match.

Because spellF1 counts spells and not frames, a single false H--A--H flicker splits one correct spell into three wrong
ones. Our design therefore favours \emph{stability} over frame-level reactivity. A change of possession needs sustained
evidence, a short spell is treated as noise, and every threshold has a physical meaning in metres, metres per second
or seconds. We chose a rule-based approach over a learned model for three reasons. The rules forbid labels from the
provided match. Public frame-level possession labels for other matches are scarce. And with a short validation
budget, explicit rules are easier to check, debug and explain than a model.

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Used: automatic tracking data (ATD).}
We read the ATD text file and its XML metadata into dense arrays: positions of all player slots in metres (normalised
coordinates times $105.3\times68$\,m), the per-player speed channel, and the ball position. The team of each slot comes
from the XML (the first team in the file is the home team, H). Slots whose team identifier belongs to neither team,
such as referees, are excluded from all distance computations. Frame indices follow the organisers' convention
(\code{frame\_id} $=$ video frame $+1$). On the graded frames the ball is present in 59.3\% of frames, and our jump
filter removes 178 observations.

Two aggregate properties of the ATD ball shaped our method. Its track is strongly smoothed: velocity changes of at
least 5\,m/s within 3 frames occur 82 times in the whole match, against about 1\,700 per match on Metrica. And it lies
farther from the players: the median distance from the ball to the nearest player is 1.9\,m, against 0.3\,m on
Metrica. We therefore detect touches over longer windows (Section~\ref{sec:control}) and validate on Metrica with the
ball degraded to match these statistics (Section~\ref{sec:results}).

\paragraph{Used only as a sanity check: public ground truth.}
The organisers publish ground truth for one five-minute window (frames 11\,185--18\,684, 17 spells). We used it only
to confirm that the pipeline output is well formed and of the expected order of magnitude. It was printed by
\code{run\_match.py} during the control-margin sweep (Section~\ref{sec:training}), but we did not use it to choose
between variants or to set thresholds. With 17 spells, one spell moves spellF1 by about 0.06, so the window cannot
separate variants anyway.

\paragraph{Evaluated but not used.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Smart Tagging.} The 59 set pieces mark restarts and so the end of dead-ball periods. We estimated an
    upper bound for this signal on Metrica (Section~\ref{sec:rejected}). Even perfect restart times and teams at
    60\% coverage raised spellF1 by only about 0.02. Real automatic tags have timing errors and no team for throw-ins,
    so we judged the risk of a late change larger than the gain.
  \item \textbf{Homography and video.} We ran the YOLOv8 ball detector of our separate computer-vision project on every
    second frame of the tactical video and projected the detections to the pitch with the organisers' homography.
    Using the video ball instead of, or in addition to, the ATD ball raised ball coverage on graded frames from 59\%
    to 72\% and 79\%, but the median distance from the ball to the nearest player stayed at 1.8--1.9\,m and the file
    had more, shorter spells (321--327 against 298). The main diagnostic did not improve, and the run needs a GPU,
    model weights and about 1.5\,h, so the submission stays ATD-only and CPU-only. The unchanged ball--player distance
    also suggests that the offset comes from the player positions or their reference point, not from the ball track.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}
The pipeline (\code{run\_match.py}, modules in \code{geo/}) processes the whole match in six steps. All thresholds
live in a single file, \code{geo/config.py}.

\subsection{Ball control}
\label{sec:control}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ball filtering.} A short stretch of the ball track (fewer than 3 frames) that is cut off from its
    neighbours by a jump faster than 45\,m/s (plus a 1\,m tolerance) is treated as a tracking error and skipped. The
    hardest shots reach about 35\,m/s, so real kicks are kept. The filter runs twice, because removing one spike can
    reconnect its neighbours. The remaining track is smoothed with a rolling median over $\pm2$ frames, and gaps of up
    to 10 frames (0.4\,s) between trusted observations are interpolated linearly.
  \item \textbf{Team distances.} For each frame we compute the distance from the ball to the nearest home player and
    to the nearest away player. The distance is infinite when the ball or a team is missing.
  \item \textbf{Control.} A team controls the ball in a frame when its nearest player lies within $R=2$\,m of the ball,
    the nearest rival is at least 0.2\,m farther, \emph{and} the ball is not passing by faster than 12\,m/s (median of
    the ball speed over $\pm2$ frames). In every other case nobody controls the ball: loose ball, contested ball, ball
    in flight, or no data. On the competition match the control condition holds in 29.0\% of the frames of play.
  \item \textbf{Touches.} A touch is a change of the ball velocity vector of at least 3\,m/s between the 12 frames
    (0.48\,s) before and after a frame, at a local peak of that change, with one team's nearest player within 3.5\,m
    and the nearest rival at least 0.5\,m farther. The long window and the large radius follow from the smoothed and
    offset ATD ball (Section~\ref{sec:data}); they were chosen on ATD-like Metrica (Section~\ref{sec:training}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dead-ball detection}
A frame is dead (D) when one of three detectors fires:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ball out of play.} The ball is more than 0.5\,m beyond a touchline or goal line. The frame stays dead
    until the ball is observed back on the pitch, including frames where the ball is missing in between (a throw-in
    is often taken while the ball is not tracked).
  \item \textbf{Projected exit.} When the ball disappears while moving faster than 3\,m/s towards a line, stays missing
    for at least 2\,s, and its next observation lies within 6\,m of that line or beyond the pitch, we extrapolate its
    last velocity for at most 1\,s and mark D from the predicted crossing.
  \item \textbf{Low collective activity.} The median speed of the tracked players, smoothed with a $\pm1$\,s rolling
    median, stays below 1.1\,m/s for at least 3\,s. Such a stretch is extended in both directions while the smoothed
    activity stays below 1.7\,m/s, because players slow down gradually and the strict threshold alone fires late.
    A trusted ball rolling faster than 2\,m/s marks play, not a stoppage.
\end{enumerate}
Gaps shorter than 1\,s between low-activity periods are closed, and a fragment of live play shorter than 2\,s between
two dead-ball periods of any kind is also D, so that no micro-spell remains between two stoppages. All frames between
the start of grading and the second-half kick-off are D.

\subsection{Possession tracking}
Within each continuous stretch of live play we track the team in possession with a decaying turnover counter. The idea
comes from the \code{PossessionTracker} in Michał Mróz's engineering thesis (a football analytics system for the
Wisła Kraków academy), adapted here to metric ATD distances.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The first team in control after a restart opens the stretch, and its possession is backfilled to the restart.
  \item Each frame controlled by the rival adds $+1$ to the counter, each frame controlled by the holder adds $-1$
    (never below zero), and frames without control leave it unchanged. When the counter reaches 13 frames (0.5\,s),
    possession changes. The change is dated back to the frame in which the rival's advantage began, not the frame in
    which the threshold was crossed.
  \item Two touches by the rival within 3\,s, with no touch by the holder in between (for example an interception
    followed by a pass), also change possession, dated from the first of them. A single rival touch, such as a
    deflection, changes nothing.
  \item A stretch of live play with no control at all inherits the team of the previous stretch.
  \item A spell shorter than 2\,s inside a stretch reverts to the previous team: the rival did not keep the ball, so
    the change was not a controlled turnover. A spell shorter than 2\,s at the very start of a stretch is given to the
    next team instead, which removes micro-spells caused by a rival standing closest to the ball at a restart.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Composing the submission}
Frames outside the two halves get X. Graded frames never get X, because the scorer counts X as an error in macroF1.
Dead ball gives D, and a graded live frame with no possession evidence at all also gives D (0.0\% of graded frames on
the competition match). The labels are computed twice and the run is accepted only if the SHA-256 hashes of both
outputs are identical. The final file is then checked with the organisers' \code{validate\_submission.py}.

  frozen. \\

Before kick-off of the 2nd half & D on all graded frames. \\

\section{Training data}
\label{sec:training}
The pipeline contains \textbf{no learned model} and uses no training data. Labelled data was used only for validation
and for setting a few thresholds:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metrica Sports Sample Games 1 and 2}
    (\url{https://github.com/metrica-sports/sample-data}). These are two anonymised professional matches, \emph{not}
    the provided match, with player and ball tracking at 25\,fps and synchronised event data annotated manually by
    Metrica Sports. Together they give about 282\,000 graded frames and 504 reference possession spells.
  \item \textbf{How we turned events into H/A/D labels} (\code{geo/metrica.py}). We did this mechanically, with no
    manual labelling by our team:
    \begin{itemize}
      \item Possession belongs to the team of the last on-ball event (pass, recovery, set piece, shot, ball lost, ball
        out). Challenges, fouls and cards do not change it.
      \item A turnover counts only if it is controlled: the new team passes or shoots, or keeps the ball for at least
        3\,s. We chose the 3\,s value so that the mean spell length of the reference ($\approx$14\,s) matches the
        published mean of the organisers' public window (14.2\,s). This affects the validation reference, not the
        pipeline.
      \item D runs from the end of the last event before a restart to the restart.
    \end{itemize}
    The result is an approximation of the organisers' ground truth, which is derived from corrected tracking.
  \item \textbf{ATD-like Metrica} (\code{metrica.atd\_like}). A copy of each Metrica match in which the ball track is
    smoothed with a 25-frame moving average and shifted by a slowly varying random offset (white noise smoothed twice
    over 10\,s, scaled to 1\,m), with four fixed seeds per match. This reproduces the two ATD statistics of
    Section~\ref{sec:data}: the median ball--player distance becomes 1.9\,m and 53\% of balls lie within 2\,m of a
    player (ATD 52.5\%).
  \item \textbf{Tuned on Metrica:} the ball-speed limit for control (12\,m/s), the minimum spell length (2\,s), the
    out-of-play margin (0.5\,m), the activity extension threshold (1.7\,m/s) and the rolling-ball threshold
    (2\,m/s) on the original tracking; the touch window, radius and velocity threshold (12 frames, 3.5\,m, 3\,m/s) as
    the best of 24 settings on ATD-like Metrica (\code{python validate\_metrica.py --sweep --atd-like}). The ball
    smoothing, the 0.4\,s gap filling and the projected exit were checked on Metrica. The control margin was chosen on
    Metrica by a sweep over 0--0.6\,m in steps of 0.05\,m; all values lie within the noise level of each other, and we
    kept 0.2\,m. For each value we also ran \code{run\_match.py} to check that the full-match file stayed valid; its
    output includes the public-window sanity check, but the choice was based on Metrica alone. The remaining
    thresholds (for example the 45\,m/s ball-jump limit, the 2\,m control radius and the 13-frame turnover counter)
    come from physical reasoning or from our earlier projects.
\end{itemize}
No labels from the provided match were used for training, tuning or model selection, and the private ground truth
was never used.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Turnovers while the ball is missing or offset.} This is the main weakness. When the ball disappears
    during a duel and reappears with the other team, or lies between players, we often miss the change or date it
    late. The ATD-like stress test shows the effect (Table~\ref{tab:stress}), and so does the public window, where we
    predict 15 spells of 18.8\,s against 17 spells of 14.2\,s. Touch detection recovers part of these turnovers, but
    geometry of the players alone did not (Table~\ref{tab:rejected}).
  \item \textbf{Short stoppages.} On Metrica we miss 10--12 whole stoppages per match, mostly quick free kicks after
    fouls, where players keep moving and the ball stays on the pitch. Stoppages shorter than 3\,s are never D.
  \item \textbf{Restart rules are not modelled.} The team that restarts is simply the first one in control. A rival
    standing closest to the ball at a throw-in can win a short spell, although the minimum-spell rule removes most of
    these cases.
  \item \textbf{Nearest player in crowds.} In a crowded penalty area the nearest player is not necessarily the one
    playing the ball. Ball height is not used to rule out headers and aerial balls passing over a player.
  \item \textbf{Tracking errors.} Identity swaps, a second ball and pitch invasions are not handled. If the ball
    disappears after leaving the pitch, out-of-play D lasts until it is seen on the pitch again. The longest such
    period on the competition match is 139\,s, and we could not verify whether it is a real stoppage.
  \item \textbf{Validation gap.} Our reference is built from events and is easier than ATD: the baseline scores 0.43
    on original Metrica and 0.35 on ATD-like Metrica, but 0.24 on the full competition match. Absolute Metrica scores
    therefore overestimate our score on the competition match. Comparisons between variants are what we rely on.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{With more time} we would:
\begin{itemize}
  \item validate on matches with ground truth of the same type as the organisers', such as the DFL/IDSSE open data,
    which provides ball status and possession per frame;
  \item use Smart Tagging restarts once they can be validated;
  \item infer the ball position during gaps from player movement (e.g.\ Kim et al., KDD 2023);
  \item find the source of the 1.9\,m ball--player offset in ATD (player reference point or projection), which video
    ball detection did not remove (Section~\ref{sec:data}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}
The code is public at \url{https://github.com/Todzaa/The_Boys_AGH_MTHA_2027} and runs on any CPU with Python~3.12,
NumPy~2.4.4 and pandas~2.3.3 (pinned in \code{requirements.txt}). No GPU, video or network access is needed at run
time.

\noindent The repository expects the following layout (paths can be overridden by environment variables):
\begin{itemize}
  \item the organisers' data in \code{../dane\_od\_organizatorow} (\code{MTAH\_DATA\_DIR});
  \item the organisers' repository with the scorer in \code{../more-than-a-hack-2027} (\code{MTAH\_REPO});
  \item for validation only, the Metrica sample data in \code{../metrica\_sample\_data} (\code{METRICA\_DIR}).
\end{itemize}

\noindent Commands:
\begin{verbatim}
pip install -r requirements.txt
python -m unittest                            # unit tests on synthetic data
python run_match.py                           # -> outputs/match/the_boys_agh.csv
python validate_metrica.py                    # validation on the two Metrica matches
python validate_metrica.py --atd-like         # the same with the ATD-like ball
python validate_metrica.py --sweep --atd-like # grid used to choose the touch thresholds
\end{verbatim}
\code{run\_match.py} computes the labels twice and requires identical SHA-256 hashes. It then runs the organisers'
validator and prints the public-window sanity check. All parameters are in \code{geo/config.py}. The full run takes
under a minute on a laptop CPU; the first run also builds the ATD cache.
